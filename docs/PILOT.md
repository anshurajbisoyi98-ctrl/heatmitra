# HeatMitra: 2-week field pilot plan

The back-test checks HeatMitra's warnings against weather data. It doesn't show that workers use it, that the effort is low enough, or that the heat estimate matches the site. This pilot is designed to answer those questions before any wider rollout.

## Setting

- **Site:** one construction or logistics site in the UAE, in summer (June to September).
- **People:** 20–40 outdoor workers in 2–4 crews, their foremen, the site supervisor and the first-aider.
- **Length:** 2 weeks of normal shifts, after a 1-day setup.
- **Equipment:** one on-site WBGT meter at the main work area (logged every 10 minutes). Optional: 8–10 chest-strap heart-rate sensors for volunteers.

## Setup day

1. The supervisor imports the crews (`name, phone, language, task, clothing, buddy`) in Crew setup and prints the join QR and rest-station posters.
2. Workers join with their phone number at the morning briefing. Workers with basic phones are set to SMS, WhatsApp or voice calls; workers with no phone are set to "No phone (foreman)".
3. The supervisor signs off site readiness, including the cold-water tub, and enters the first WBGT meter reading to calibrate the estimate.
4. Consent is explained in each worker's language by someone they trust (not their supervisor). Taking part is voluntary and has no effect on pay or work.

## What we measure

| Question | Measure | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Do workers answer? | Share of check-ins answered by the worker (in the app, from the notification, by SMS/WhatsApp/call) | ≥ 80% |
| Is the effort low enough? | Prompts per worker per shift; taps per shift; share answered from the lock screen | ≤ 10 prompts per hot shift |
| Do foremen use crew check? | Crew checks per shift; share of check-ins closed by crew check | used every day |
| Does help come fast? | Median time from a missed check-in to a human response | < 5 min |
| Is the estimate right? | Mean and worst error of estimated WBGT vs the on-site meter, before and after calibration | mean error ≤ 1.5 °C after calibration |
| Do breaks happen? | Rest breaks confirmed ÷ rest breaks due; break-check score; station audits done | rest adherence ≥ 80% |
| Is it safe? | Heat-related first-aid cases and outcomes, compared with the same weeks last season (site records) | no increase |
| Is it fair? | All of the above split by language and by channel (app, text, voice, no phone) | no group much worse |
| Do workers trust it? | 3 questions at the end, in their language, by voice if needed: "Was it easy?", "Did it help you rest?", "Did you worry it would be used against you?" | most say easy and helpful; few worried |

## Safety and ethics

- HeatMitra supports existing site rules; it never replaces the supervisor's duty of care, the midday ban or the first-aider.
- No data is used for discipline or pay. Supervisors see crew-level figures; symptoms go only to the first-aider, with consent.
- Anonymous reports go to the HSE manager, not only the site supervisor.
- Data is kept for the pilot and deleted after the report, except the anonymous crew-level summary.
- Any heat-illness case is handled by the site's normal first-aid and emergency process, and reviewed afterwards.

## Decisions at the end

- Keep, shorten or lengthen the check-in interval (now hourly, 30 min for workers at higher risk).
- Whether voice calls or WhatsApp work better than the app for each language group.
- Whether the WBGT estimate needs a permanent on-site sensor at this kind of site.
- Whether the kidney-check prompt threshold (100 hours in high heat a season) should change, with the occupational-health partner.
